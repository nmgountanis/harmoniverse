What Am I Looking At?

The spectrum shows how much acoustic power is present at every frequency in the sound right now, from a rumbling low note to a bright, high overtone. Peaks correspond to strong, resonant frequencies; a clean musical tone produces a tall peak at its fundamental frequency, plus a series of smaller peaks at whole-number multiples of it, its harmonics.

The analyzer estimates the fundamental frequency using autocorrelation, a technique that compares the waveform against time-shifted copies of itself to find its true repeating period, which tends to be far more accurate for musical pitch than simply picking the tallest spectrum peak. From that fundamental, it predicts where each harmonic should sit and measures how strong each one actually is, which is what the harmonic bars show.

Cents measure pitch distance on a scale where 100 cents equal one semitone. The needle shows how far your note sits from the nearest note in standard 12-tone equal temperament (A4 = 440 Hz), which is useful for tuning an instrument or checking intonation precisely.
