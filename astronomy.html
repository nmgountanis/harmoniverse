Harmonics (ℓ, m) vs. radial order (n) — and how the four panels connect ℓ and m describe the angular pattern — the shape of ripples across the surface, drawn in the "Surface pattern" panel. Radial order n describes something the surface can't show you: how many ripples the same wave makes going inward, toward the center — visible only in the "Interior cross-section" panel. The physics behind the panels

These are the scaling relations and asymptotic formulas driving the four panels above. They are the standard textbook relations used in solar-like and red-giant asteroseismology, simplified where noted for a live, interactive display.

Frequency of maximum power, νmax

Sets where oscillation power peaks in the spectrum. It scales with surface gravity g = GM/R² and, more weakly, with Teff through the acoustic cutoff frequency. Bigger, cooler, lower-gravity red giants oscillate at far lower frequency than the Sun.

Large frequency separation, Δν

The spacing between consecutive radial-order overtones of the same degree ℓ. It is set by the sound travel time across the star, so it scales as the square root of the mean stellar density.

Asymptotic p-mode frequencies

Radial order n and angular degree ℓ combine into a near-regular frequency comb, offset by a small phase term ε and a small ℓ-dependent correction δν0ℓ. This is why the power spectrum shows evenly spaced ridges.

Surface displacement pattern

The radial displacement is a real spherical harmonic Yℓm. ℓ is the total number of nodal lines on the surface; |m| of them are longitude lines through the poles, and the rest are latitude circles. Selecting several modes sums several such terms.

Rotational splitting

Rotation breaks the (2ℓ+1)-fold degeneracy in azimuthal order m, splitting each mode into a multiplet spaced by roughly the rotation frequency Ω/2π, scaled by a structure-dependent factor Cnℓ near 1 for p-modes.

g-mode period spacing, ΔΠ₁

In evolved stars, gravity waves trapped near the dense core are evenly spaced in period, not frequency, set by the buoyancy (Brunt–Väisälä) frequency N integrated through the radiative core. This mixes with p-modes to make the "mixed modes" seen in red giants.

This tool uses a simplified stand-in, ΔΠ₁ ≈ 77·(1−fconv)/√(M/R³) minutes, to mimic how a deeper envelope lowers ΔΠ₁ — not the full mode-physics integral above.

Granulation noise (Harvey profile)

Convective granulation on the surface produces slowly evolving brightness variations well described by this profile: most of its power sits at low frequency, so it appears as a wandering baseline rather than sharp peaks, and must be modeled out to reveal the oscillation spectrum.
